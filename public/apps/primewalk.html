Consecutive last digits

Given one prime's last digit (rows), how often the next prime ends in each digit (columns). A truly random sequence would show 25% everywhere.

Rows: this prime. Columns: the next prime. Repeats on the diagonal are rarer, the bias Oliver and Soundararajan described, which the authors link to the walk's compactness.
Rules, as in the paper: the number 1 sits at (0, 0). Each following number stays on the same point unless it is prime, in which case the walker moves up for a last digit of 1, down for 3, left for 7 and right for 9. The primes 2 and 5 cause no step. The random walk comparison moves at exactly the same moments but picks each direction at random. Primes are generated with a segmented sieve of Eratosthenes in your browser.
