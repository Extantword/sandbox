Consecutive last digits

Given one prime's last digit (rows), how often the next prime ends in each digit (columns). A truly random sequence would show 25% everywhere.

Rows: this prime. Columns: the next prime. Repeats on the diagonal are rarer, the bias Oliver and Soundararajan described, which the authors link to the walk's compactness.
Rules, after Fraile, Kinouchi, Dwivedi, Martínez, Raptis & Fernández, “Prime numbers and random walks in a square grid” (2021): the number 1 sits at (0, 0). Each following number stays on the same point unless it is prime, in which case the walker moves up for a last digit of 1, down for 3, left for 7 and right for 9. The primes 2 and 5 cause no step. The random walk moves at exactly the same moments but picks each direction at random. The counting never stops: primes come from a segmented sieve of Eratosthenes that extends itself as the numbers grow.
